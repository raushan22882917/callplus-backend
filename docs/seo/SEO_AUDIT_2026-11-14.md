# SEO audit: callplus.autobot360.com (14 November 2026)

Method: eight specialist audits (technical, content, schema, sitemap, AI search, agent readiness, visual and mobile, performance) run against the live site with the claude-seo toolkit and Chromium. All 26 sitemap URLs were fetched. Core Web Vitals are **lab measurements** from local Chromium; Google PageSpeed was rate-limited, so there is no field data. Detailed findings per specialist are in the scratchpad audit folder (technical.md, content.md, schema.md, sitemap.md, geo.md, agentic.md, visual.md, performance.md).

## Biggest problems

1. **The site states the price two ways.** The homepage FAQ (visible text and its FAQ markup) prices in "credits" at 2.50, 4-5, 8-9 and 17.50 a minute. /pricing, the pricing answer page and llms.txt say ₹5 to ₹4 answered and ₹9 to ₹8 outbound. The homepage also says "No monthly fee" while plans cost ₹2,999 to ₹54,999 a month.
2. **Conflicting claims about phone numbers.** Most pages say CallPlus does not sell numbers; the homepage hero says Indian numbers come "out of the box", the FAQ says "get one in the dashboard", and /docs lists an endpoint to buy a number.
3. **Thin trust signals.** /privacy is 110 words with no legal entity, data categories, retention or grievance contact. No legal name, address or team anywhere. /about is 189 words. No testimonials, case studies or sample calls.
4. **Weak Bing and brand presence** (Copilot and ChatGPT draw on Bing): no Bing verification, no IndexNow submissions, one dev.to profile link, Organization not tied to Autobot360 as parent.
5. **Structured data gaps:** a retired HowTo on /answers/set-up-callplus; no JSON-LD on /pricing, /about, /contact, /security, /terms, /privacy, /docs; anonymous publisher nodes on answer pages.
6. **Mobile:** no header menu on the homepage at phone width; pricing table clipped; tap targets under 44 px; the hero headline and button are baked into the video (about 6 px text on mobile).
7. **Heavy hero video:** 22 MB, no mobile version, streams in the background. It does not block LCP (the poster does) but wastes mobile data.

## What works

HTTPS with HTTP/2 and redirects; all 26 URLs return 200 with self-canonicals and are in the sitemap; no orphan pages; content and JSON-LD are in the raw HTML; strong security headers; robots.txt allows the ten AI and search bots and blocks only app paths; llms.txt, llms-full.txt, security.txt present; answer pages open with a 40-60 word direct answer; CLS is 0; lab LCP is about 2.2-2.4 s on mobile; no third-party requests; agent usability 100/100.

## Other findings

- Hindi page has `<html lang="en-IN">` (hreflang pairing is correct).
- Homepage HTML has no link to /pricing; /docs has one internal link; homepage links to 4 of 16 answer pages.
- HTML sent with `s-maxage` and no `max-age`; gzip only (no Brotli); no CDN; first byte 0.6-1.5 s with variance; `www.` host does not resolve.
- All sitemap `lastmod` values are identical; robots.txt has a non-standard `Host:` line.
- Language counts differ (11 vs 10 Indian languages; correct figure is English plus 10 spoken).
- /pricing and /terms say any own key avoids the minute charge; the answer page says all three parts must be on your own keys.
- Homepage says the AI "never makes things up"; /terms says it can make mistakes.
- Optional draft standards missing: Content-Signal, Markdown delivery, WebMCP, ai-catalog.json.
- "Collections" and "Clinics" use cases carry no compliance caveat; the logo shows (R) without evidence of registration.
